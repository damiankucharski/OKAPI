# Fitting one temperature to a multiclass logarithmic pool

Frozen before fitting or scoring these banks. The historical wrong-temperature reference in the four- and five-class generators led to a more useful question for OKAPI. Can an existing single `LogitMeanNode` learn an appropriate confidence scale from a small labeled sample, and when does its assumption that all inputs carry equal weight fail? The corrected analytic posterior gives a known achievable setting on both exchangeable source laws wherever the native clamps are inactive. Our prior Gaussian-density calculation accessed the generator parameters and did not estimate a temperature. [Derivation and historical scope](../standard_point_control/multiclass_posterior.md)

## Fixed cohorts and available information

Use 16 new banks, seeds 61200–61215, at two nested fit sizes, 128 and 512 examples. Obtain a separate 256-row validation set and 4,096 fresh samples each from source IID and a frozen shifted law. The provider ID permutation is random per family and bank and fixed across splits. Input order is identical for every method, labels are the same observed class indices, and every held-out score comes after the fit rule is frozen. Each family has uniform class priors, with predictors drawn from the existing class-conditional raw-logit Gaussian law in the first two scenarios. Metric tables will include all families and both fit sizes. Pin the generator and baselines to FusionBench b93281d. Compute exact conditional targets from the Gaussian law separately, so the two old incorrect reference fields cannot enter training or measurement.

| Source family | Fixed source law | Fixed shift |
|---|---|---|
| MulticlassCommittee | 8 independent experts, 5 classes, constructor accuracy .60, equal class boost | Set the shared noise correlation to .55, preserving each expert's marginal law. |
| Saturated | 6 experts, 4 classes, constructor accuracy .95 and shared noise correlation .95 | Reduce shared noise correlation to .35, keeping class boosts fixed. |
| Heterogeneous | 8 independent experts, 5 classes. Two receive true-class raw-logit boost 2.25 and the remaining six receive .10. | Move the pair of strong boosts to two previously weak positions, keeping the source's number of strong providers and set of boosts. The anonymous permutation stays fixed. |

For the first two laws the known population scale is `K s/[1+(K−1)ρ]` on the mean log probabilities. Under the third law, the exact class log score is the sum of each provider's log probability times its individual boost. A common scale cannot recover all these independent continuously varying log-odds when boosts differ. Scan actual rows for any activation of the native input or output clamp. Report numerical agreement separately from the exact mathematical expression valid away from clamps.

## Fitting and controls

The studied tree contains the original providers in their given order under one native `LogitMeanNode`. Fit its single scalar T by minimizing multiclass negative log likelihood on the fit rows only. Let `L = mean_k(log(clip(p_k, 10⁻⁶, 1−10⁻⁶)))`. Use the native positive-temperature interval `[.05, min(50, 30/max(−L))]` so the node's output clamp at ±30 cannot change the training objective inside the fitted interval. The derivative is mean over rows of `E_softmax(TL)[L] − L_y`; the second derivative is the mean within-row variance. Use a deterministic monotone-root solver and nearest-to-one tie policy for a constant objective. Calculate held-out predictions with the actual native node and standard per-node multiclass normalization. T is the inverse of the temperature in the conventional `softmax(z/T)` notation.

Compare the following policies on the same anonymous predictions and labeled fit rows.

* The identical logarithmic tree with T fixed at one measures the effect of introducing a fitted scalar. A structure- and computation-matched random search always tries T=1 and then draws uniformly over the identical interval. Its candidate count equals the deterministic fitter's number of complete fit derivative and likelihood evaluations in that cell. Minimize fit NLL with first occurrence on a tie. CPU time is reported independently because the primitive operations differ.
* The mandatory cheap controls are the all-provider arithmetic average, FusionBench `TopN(metric='brier')` evaluating all prefixes of the individually Brier-ranked providers, and regularized multinomial logistic stacking on the public marginal-logit features (`StackingLogit`, C=1, usual training scaler). Include `StackingCLR`, C=1, which can assign different weights to each centered log-probability and admits all three analytic laws in its unregularized feature family. Preserve each public method's own normalization.
* Run ordinary native six-operator OKAPI at 600 full training fitness assessments with each of seeds 1631 and 1637. Use FusionBench's standard shallow-seeded `fit_suite_point`, three-decimal production selection and the first unrounded best-fit archived winner, with point-mutation and survivor reuse disabled. This measures ordinary search at a stated budget. The new scalar fitter, unit-temperature control and random scalar control operate on a supplied all-provider structure. Record the native archive validation cost even though the fit-selected winner uses no validation label. Neither its evaluation budget nor its grammar is equated to the scalar searches.

## Outcomes and interpretation

The primary contrast is **learned T minus StackingCLR in IID class-summed conditional-excess Brier at 128 fit rows**, averaged equally over the two exchangeable families per bank. Report a paired two-sided 95% Student interval across the 16 independent bank means. Negative favors the scalar model. Secondary outcomes include class-summed observed Brier, class-summed squared distance to the known conditional probabilities, NLL and accuracy on fit, validation, fresh IID and shift. Report each method on each family at both fit sizes, the two GP seeds' losses separately, optimizer fit-NLL versus random search, fitted T and fit/validation costs and elapsed time. Give separate paired estimates for the deliberately misspecified heterogeneous source and for individual shifts. An advantage over a high-dimensional fit at small n supports the stated restriction in that setting. The experiment makes no claim that the source model, equal provider weights or a temperature learned before a correlation shift is universally correct. No public dataset is used as an external transfer test here.

The fitting idea has a calibration precedent in Guo, Pleiss, Sun and Weinberger, *On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks* (2017), who evaluate a one-parameter temperature on held-out classifier logits. Their experiments do not establish results for logarithmically pooled anonymous multiclass experts or for the covariance and competence shifts above. Our structural restriction, Gaussian control, matched search and numerical results are separate contributions.
