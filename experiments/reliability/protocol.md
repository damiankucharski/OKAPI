# Cross-fitted pretraining audit of a provider pool

## Decision and scope

For binary probability columns, compare the equal-weight pool to each leave-one-provider-out mean with paired per-row Brier savings (full minus omitted). The proposed opt-in audit randomly splits the labeled source rows in half. In each half nominate the omission with highest overall mean saving. Evaluate that candidate's savings on the opposite half, separately in each supplied operating group. Drop at most one provider when both halves nominate the same ID and all held-out group lower bounds are positive. Missing or singleton validation groups veto removal. The pooled ablation treats all rows as one group. Keep the audit threshold and all experimental settings fixed across both seed banks.

The one-sided empirical Bernstein lower bound uses sample variance, failure probability .05/(2G) per fold/group and conservative gap range width 4/K for K providers. For a fixed trained nominee, held-out rows must be independent within group and future conditional risk stable. Positive mean gains apply to this equal-weight mean and the measured groups. Neither future fault recovery nor a downstream model with learned signs is covered.

## Frozen conditions and contrasts

Six binary providers share balanced labels. The five background providers have nominal accuracy .78, with shared Gaussian noise weight .55. Stable failure uses a sixth independent provider with reversed .85-accuracy predictions. A specialist uses a .97-accuracy detector in group 1 and defers near .5 elsewhere. The common-error control generates six exchangeable .78-accuracy providers with shared noise weight .55, then makes every provider emit one identical uninformative saturated bit on group-1 rows. Column identities are shuffled once per seed and then maintained across splits. Use FusionBench's binary calibrated detector generator.

Source and IID have group-1 prevalence .20. The mixture shift raises it to .65. An additional repaired-failure shift uses the .65 prevalence and restores the sixth provider's correct orientation after decisions freeze. Methods include keep-all mean, drop the provider with worst individual source Brier, the pooled and grouped cross-fitted audits, and supervised all-provider FusionBench logit stacking. Stacking fits on the same labels and may learn a negative coefficient. No tuning, no GP search, no target adaptation.

Use independent banks A (10300–10311) and B (10400–10411). Each seed/family draws 8,192 audit rows and 4,096 fresh rows per test domain on separate seeded streams. Fit all methods before generating test examples. The primary contrast is grouped audit minus keep-all Brier on the stable-failure IID domain, separately in each bank, with paired Student 95% intervals across seeds. Report all families, shifts, control scores, decisions and specialist-group damage, including test Brier in group 1 and the fraction of runs that drop the designated provider. Record fit/validation label budget, candidate and confirmation row evaluations, elapsed preprocessing, fit and inference times, provider count and the applicable conditions for the confidence bound. No repeated choice over test scores.
