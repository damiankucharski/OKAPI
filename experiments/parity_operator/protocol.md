# Can a fitted, binary disagreement operator recover held-out Parity?

The prior Guided transfer study found near-prevalence IID Brier on FusionBench Parity. The generator simulates two independent latent bits, each measured by several calibrated detectors, with an XOR target. Its known Bayes rule motivates this proposal. A comparison in this setting measures whether the proposed operator and blind grouping recover the intended mechanism, with one unrelated negative control for specificity. It does not establish portability to new mechanisms. [Earlier observation](../guided_transfer/README.md)

## Policies frozen before scoring

Add an optional n-ary probabilistic XOR node to OKAPI. For two independent Bernoulli events with probabilities p and q, the probability of exactly one is p(1−q)+(1−p)q. Recursing over inputs gives the probability of an odd number. Use one positive-class probability channel. The default GP operator palette is unchanged.

For the search experiment, estimate signed Pearson distances from anonymous provider outputs and cut average linkage at two groups on fit rows. The number of groups is prespecified and informed by the Parity family. Within both groups use one of two fixed pools, either the arithmetic mean or sigmoid of the summed clipped logits (OKAPI LogitMean with temperature equal to group size). At the root use one of four fixed operators. The first three are MIN, MAX and mean. The fourth is the new XOR in the candidate arm, or ordinary logit mean with temperature one and shift zero in the comparison arm. Fit all eight real trees, choose the first best fit Brier and freeze it. Also fit both arms on the same group-size-preserving uniform provider permutation for each of two prespecified starts (311, 751). The inferred-group arms are deterministic. There is no label-dependent grouping and no model IDs, group metadata, test correlations or oracle outputs available to any fitting policy.

The two ordinary arithmetic/logit group rules and each of the four root operators are shared across families. Do not tune temperatures, class thresholds, number of groups or prefix sizes on validation. No validation selection or refinement. Score every arm's eight templates, including duplicates or worse candidates. Treat the native default GP as an additional contextual comparison at its own larger budget of 160 scored trees and two fixed starts (311, 751). Use stock defaults and singleton initialization as in the guided driver, with exact structural deduplication and the same best-full-precision-fit archive. If the full 640-call historical harness is used, identify the 160-point and 640-point costs separately.

## Data, inference and costs

Use 12 independent fresh banks 30400–30411 for each of FusionBench Parity and CorrelatedRedundancy, with the same anonymized provider permutation across all four splits in each bank. Reuse the frozen study data function with 384 fit, 256 validation, 4,096 IID and 4,096 shifted test rows. Parity detector accuracy drops from .92 to .78. The CorrelatedRedundancy shift reduces dependency in the redundant block. Fit the arithmetic mean of all providers (zero learned parameters), public ranked-prefix Top-N Brier and C=1 logistic regression on all provider logits to matched inputs. The learned controls, templates and ordinary GP see exactly the same fit labels. Top-N scores all individual providers and all unweighted prefixes, using only fit to determine both order and N. Freeze all candidates and references before scoring held-out splits.

Primary comparison is grouped XOR-candidate minus grouped substitute-candidate IID Brier on Parity, lower preferred. Report unadjusted paired 95% t intervals over banks, averaging the two starts for stochastic arms. Report all methods across all four splits, including the unrelated family. Record operator, chosen pool, anonymous group assignment or its reproducible seed, choice frequencies, complexity, selected N, fit calls/timing and held-out prediction time. Keep ordinary GP's different evaluation count visible. The recovered tree is achievable with this augmented library. The generator's use of exactly the same binary gate and independent detector mechanism is a strong favorable condition.

As a read-only diagnostic after freezing the models, replay the generator's published analytic posterior (it knows the true group assignment) and source-fit prevalence on the Parity banks. Compare their four-split Brier with the inferred-group winner. The posterior is a population reference and can have higher empirical loss than a fitted candidate on finite samples. Neither reference enters the candidate search.
