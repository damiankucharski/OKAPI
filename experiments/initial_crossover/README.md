# Was the hidden rule already one crossover away?

On the eight-provider two-pathway task, ordinary GP found the planted rule after initialization in 20 of 24 runs starting from branched templates. In **12 of those 20** the initial population could already construct it by a single native crossover of target-free parents. In the remaining **eight**, every legal initial swap missed it. The latter runs required something beyond a single unmutated crossover of the original parents. The archived outcome identifies discovery by 1,200 training calls; it does not identify the actual ancestry of a later hit. ¹ The mean includes all 24 starting populations and excludes target-holding parent pairs. An event is a native crossover producing at least one target child. Swapping two target-free parents in the one BRANCH population that already contained the rule elsewhere still had positive probability. Among the 23 initially absent BRANCH starts, the mean per-draw probability was 0.464%, and in all three that never reached the target at 1,200 calls it was zero.

The paired 12-bank native probability difference for BRANCH minus CHAIN was **+0.472 percentage points [0.147, 0.796]**. With uniformly sampled parents the difference was +0.071 points [0.024, 0.118]. The two-sided paired intervals average both search starts per bank. On the chain-law control the native difference was **−0.100 points [−0.214, +0.014]**. Seven of 24 BRANCH populations and four of 24 CHAIN populations on that control had a direct route, but three and one of those, respectively, still missed the target at 1,200 evaluations. No state had a uniform-parent route entirely suppressed by the actual five-way tournaments. The initial 38 parents included the eight individual providers and 30 scored seeds. Programs could encode the same function in several slots. Our enumeration retains each slot and its actual rounded fitness, includes self mating and checks both children over the full 256 binary corners. For pure MIN/MAX with identity postprocessing these corners determine the function on the entire continuous cube. Native crossover was uncapped. Search could also perform mutation and produce new parent populations after this point. The observed association between an initial one-step opportunity and eventual success cannot measure which operation produced a specific winning program.

No new search was trained. We recomputed 5,472 source-fit scores for the 144 frozen populations, checked all 4,320 serialized initial proposals plus the singletons, and reproduced each initial fit winner. The underlying native benchmark's validation-selected model scored .19047 IID positive-channel Brier for BRANCH and .19991 for CHAIN on the branched law. The independent arithmetic, Top-N and logistic baselines, validation and shifted results use the original matched predictions and rows. An independent checker reconstructed the six distinct G0 genotype lists from their archived structural keys, ran all 4,446 unordered parent pairs through actual OKAPI crossover and evaluated 123,070 child truth tables across 61,535 sampling branches. All 144 native and uniform probabilities matched the symbolic computation exactly as fractions.
